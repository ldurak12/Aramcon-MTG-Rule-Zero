Why not just use brackets?

The official Commander Brackets are a great starting point. They give everyone shared vocabulary for a deck’s power. But they rate the deck on one scale, and that creates blind spots:

  • Play experience gets folded into power. A fragile, all-in Bracket 4 deck in the hands of a newer player plays nothing like an optimized one piloted by a veteran, yet both wear the same number.
  • Some decks don’t fit anywhere. Jank piles stuffed with Game Changers, and tuned low-power decks run by sharp pilots, fall through the cracks.
  • Brackets are a checklist, not a feel. Counting cards doesn’t tell the table how fast, how interactive, or how serious a game will be.

Our thesis: power and play experience are strongly correlated, but one doesn’t cause the other. So we ask two questions instead of one. Tap any glowing region below.

Using it at the table

  • Each person places their deck and their mood tonight on the map.
  • If everyone lands in the same region or neighbors, play.
  • If not, talk it through and adjust the games to fit.
